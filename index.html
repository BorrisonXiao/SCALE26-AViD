SCALE 2026 · AViD

Verifying claims against video, one part of the parse at a time

AViD checks whether a claim holds in a video-with-audio and, more importantly, decomposes the verdict: which part of the claim is grounded, by which evidence channel — something you can see, on-screen text, speech, or a non-speech sound — and where the composition breaks. The engine is a conditional random field over the claim's dependency parse tree.

Read the method note

A slightly better yes/no is not the interesting result — a whole-claim verdict is ~84% redundant across audio and visual. The structure is.

The idea in one paragraph

Label every node of the parse, then reconcile

Parse the claim into a dependency tree. Ask a multimodal model, for each node on its own, whether that part is grounded in the clip — and by which channel. Then run belief propagation over the tree, trading each part's own evidence against a small set of composition rules (a required part cannot be missing, a negation flips its scope, a coordination needs both or either). The root's reconciled label is the verdict, and it is faithful to the parts by construction: the sub-part labels actually compose into it. That is the property flat, part-by-part fact-checking cannot give you — and the reason it silently mis-handles negation and binding.

Where the gain comes from

The tree beats the flat baseline on every perturbation type — negation 88.1% vs 84.4%, entity-swap 93.0% vs 75.5%, argument-removal 71.1% vs 31.1% — because a whole-claim model reading a mangled claim does not notice a piece is missing, while a compositional model checks each required part and its binding directly.
